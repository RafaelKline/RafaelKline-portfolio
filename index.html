Sustainable design · Structures

Sustainable Timber Home, Monash VIC

CIVE1155 · 2026 · Individual

Designed a two-storey timber-framed home on a 560 m² sloping lot, checked it against Victorian planning standards, and used energy modelling to test material choices.

8.2 starsFirstRate5 rating
54%demand offset by rainwater
3.4 starscore-filled block alternative
More detail
  • Passive design: north-oriented living areas, 600 mm eaves, double-glazed windows and deciduous planting.
  • Annual heating 34.5 and cooling 6.5 MJ/m². Brick veneer outperformed core-filled concrete block by a wide margin.
  • Roof catchment of about 227 m² harvests roughly 118 kL a year against a 219 kL household demand.
  • Embodied energy review using YourHome data: concrete, carpet and steel identified as the main contributors.
  • Part 2: member checks to AS 1720.1 (floor joist, truss bottom chord, stud, lintel), foundation cut-and-fill balance and framing drawings.
Tools: FirstRate5, AutoCAD, Excel, SpaceGass

Structural analysis

Statically Indeterminate Truss: Analysis and Load Test

CIVE1143 · 2025 · Individual

Analysed a braced timber truss by hand, checked the results in Excel, then built a scale timber model and tested it under water loads.

0.35 mmpredicted deflection
3load cases tested
More detail
  • Member forces by method of joints with a compatibility check for the redundant member.
  • The predicted deflection was below measurable range, matching the zero deflection observed in the test.
  • The frame is 400 mm long and 150 mm high with X-bracing, which makes it statically indeterminate.
  • Excel was used to tabulate member forces and lengths for the deflection calculation.
  • The model was loaded by hanging a bag of water from the loaded joint, with a ruler used to check for movement.
  • The report covers the hand calculations, model photos, test results and a discussion of why no deflection was measurable.
Tools: hand calculations, Excel, physical model
